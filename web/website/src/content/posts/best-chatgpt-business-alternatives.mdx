ChatGPT Business is OpenAI's shared AI workspace for teams. It brings ChatGPT, research, connected applications and agentic work together with centralized billing and administrative controls. Teams can use it to analyze information, create documents, write code and work with company knowledge.

You may want an alternative when your team needs several model providers, AI built into its existing office suite, or a workspace for building its own agents. Other reasons include adding those agents to Slack, inspecting their costs, versioning their configuration or hosting the system on your own infrastructure.

The options below cover those different needs. Some replace the everyday team assistant. Others change how you build and run AI across the business.

## What is ChatGPT Business?

[ChatGPT Business](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8792828-what-is-chatgpt-business) is a self-serve plan for organizations that want a shared ChatGPT workspace. It was previously called ChatGPT Team.

Standard seats include ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex, alongside features such as Projects, GPTs, apps, company knowledge and deep research. Administrators manage membership, billing and access, with usage visibility and spend controls. OpenAI does not train on Business workspace data.

Standard seats cost $20 per user per month with annual billing, or $25 with monthly billing. Premium seats cost $100 per user per month annually, or $125 monthly, with higher usage limits. A workspace requires at least two Standard or Premium seats in total. API usage is billed separately.

The appeal is getting OpenAI's products in one managed workspace. People already familiar with ChatGPT can continue using it, while the business manages access and payment centrally.

## Why consider a ChatGPT Business alternative?

The question is what you want to change about your team's AI setup.

### You want more choice of models

ChatGPT Business centers on OpenAI's products. A multi-model workspace lets your team use models from different providers without treating each provider as a separate workplace.

That can matter when one model fits a research task and another fits writing or a recurring extraction job. It also lets you change providers as your requirements change.

### You want to build agents around recurring responsibilities

A weekly competitor report has more behind it than one prompt. It needs a source list, company context, instructions, connected tools, an output format and a review process.

An agent workspace lets you package those parts into a coworker that the team can reuse and improve. Look at how the system stores the configuration, shares it, records changes and runs the work again.

### You want agents in the channels your team uses

Connecting an AI product to Slack data is different from adding an agent to a Slack channel and asking it to work there.

If Slack is where requests and reviews happen, check whether colleagues can mention the agent, receive its response in a thread and continue the work together. The interaction matters as much as the integration logo.

### You want control over deployment and costs

Some teams want a hosted service. Others need to operate the workspace themselves, choose their model credentials or inspect the configuration behind each agent.

Cost also has several parts: seats, model calls, credits, runtime and infrastructure. A plan's monthly price does not tell you what one recurring agent will cost to operate.

## How to choose a ChatGPT Business alternative

Start with one job your team already does. A client proposal, weekly research report or support handoff is more useful than a generic test prompt.

Then compare five things:

- **Where the work happens.** A separate AI workspace, an office document or a Slack channel.
- **What the team shares.** Agents, instructions, files, conversations, connected tools and permissions.
- **How work repeats.** Reusable configurations, scheduled tasks and application-triggered runs.
- **What you control.** Models, credentials, agent versions and deployment.
- **What you pay for.** Seats, model usage, runtime, integrations and hosting.

These questions separate products that can produce similar answers but fit different operating needs.

## 1. Agenta: best for building shared AI coworkers and automations

[Agenta](https://agenta.ai/) is an open-source, low-code workspace where teams build AI coworkers and automations through conversation. Each coworker can have its own job, instructions, skills, files, tools, permissions and schedule.

Instead of giving every task to the same general assistant, you can create a competitor-research agent, a support agent and a reporting agent. Your team can share those agents and their conversations, work on their files and improve how they operate.

### Add your agents to Slack

You can [publish an Agenta agent to Slack](https://agenta.ai/docs/guides/connect-slack), invite it to a channel and let teammates mention it there. The agent responds in a thread. Direct messages are also supported.

For example, a teammate could ask a research coworker in `#market-research` to summarize a competitor's announcements and prepare a report. The conversation and result stay available in the thread for the team to discuss.

This is more than letting an assistant search Slack messages. You are bringing a configured coworker into the place where people request and review work.

### Version the agent as you improve it

Agenta records changes to agent configuration. Your team can compare versions to see what changed in its instructions, skills or tools, rather than relying on a conversation to explain why the agent now behaves differently.

If the research coworker repeatedly misses an important source, you can update its instructions and save a new configuration version. The improvement becomes part of the agent you reuse, not just a correction to one report.

Version history also gives a technical teammate a place to inspect changes made by the people using the agent. See the guide to [prompt and configuration versioning](/blog/prompt-versioning-guide).

### Choose your model and how you access it

Agenta supports Claude Code, Codex and Pi as harnesses, the programs that run the agent's model-and-tool loop. Different harnesses support different models, giving you options across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and other providers.

You can change the supported model and provider configuration while retaining the agent's instructions, skills, files and tools. That separates the coworker you build from a single model vendor.

You can also [use an existing ChatGPT subscription](https://agenta.ai/docs/guides/use-your-own-subscription) on a local, self-hosted Agenta deployment. This route uses your own harness login rather than a model API key. It is for a deployment you operate for yourself, not a shared login for an entire team. Subscription access is not available through Agenta Cloud's documented setup.

### See usage and estimated cost

Agenta's tracing and analytics let you inspect model and tool calls and track requests, tokens, latency and estimated cost. When an agent's work becomes expensive or slow, you can examine the run rather than only looking at the final answer.

That gives you a basis for adjusting the model, instructions or tools used for a recurring job. Estimated model cost is not the same as a complete bill: subscriptions, hosting and connected services may contribute additional costs.

### Use the cloud or self-host

You can use Agenta Cloud or [run Agenta on your own infrastructure](https://agenta.ai/docs/self-host/overview). The open-source core lets you inspect and modify the workspace rather than depend entirely on one hosted product.

**Best for:** Teams and agencies building shared agents for recurring work, especially when Slack access, configuration versioning, cost visibility and deployment choice matter.

**Pricing:** The open-source core is free to self-host; model and infrastructure costs still apply. [Agenta Cloud](https://agenta.ai/pricing) has a free Hobby plan. Starter costs $29 per month and includes 2,900 monthly credits and unlimited team members. Additional usage consumes credits; paid Cloud plans do not charge per seat.

**Pros**

- Build and improve agents through conversation.
- Share coworkers, conversations and files with your team.
- Add agents to Slack channels and direct messages.
- Inspect configuration versions, runs, usage and estimated costs.
- Choose supported models and harnesses.
- Self-host, including supported personal subscription access, or use Agenta Cloud.

**Cons**

- Tools, instructions and permissions need initial configuration.
- Self-hosting means operating and maintaining the deployment.
- Subscription access and model compatibility depend on the runtime setup.

**Verdict:** Choose Agenta when you want to build coworkers your team can use, inspect and improve, with control over the models and infrastructure behind them.

## 2. Claude Team: best for teams that want Claude for documents, analysis and coding

[Claude Team](https://claude.com/pricing) brings Anthropic's assistant, document tools and coding capabilities into a managed team workspace. It includes shared projects, skills, connectors, enterprise search, Claude Code and Cowork capabilities, with centralized billing and administration.

A team can keep project material together, work through documents and connect business tools without building an agent platform from scratch. The same plan supports people preparing reports and developers working with Claude Code.

The main choice is the model ecosystem. Claude Team keeps the team within Anthropic's product and model family. It is not a way to give everyone a choice of OpenAI, Anthropic and Google models in the same workspace.

## 3. Microsoft 365 Copilot: best for teams working in Microsoft 365

That changes where people use AI. A finance team can work in its spreadsheet, a sales team can prepare a presentation and an operations team can use meeting context without moving every task into a separate chat application.

Copilot also includes research and analysis agents. More specialized agent-building needs can involve Copilot Studio, so the right comparison is the combination of products and licenses you need, not the Copilot name alone.

**Pricing:** Copilot is available through per-user add-ons and Microsoft 365 bundles. Add-on pricing sits alongside the required Microsoft 365 subscription; bundles combine those costs. Business and enterprise offers, billing periods and promotions differ.

**Pros**

- AI inside familiar office applications.
- Access to work context across Microsoft 365.
- Research and analysis agents alongside document assistance.
- Administration within the Microsoft environment.

**Cons**

- Licensing requires distinguishing the add-on, base plan and bundles.
- Custom agent scenarios can introduce further services and usage costs.

For a team that already works in Google Workspace, this makes AI part of the existing document and communication workflow. It also changes the cost comparison: Workspace includes email, storage and collaboration software, not just an AI assistant.

## How to move one workflow from ChatGPT Business

Choose a recurring task and collect the parts it depends on: instructions, reference files, examples, connected applications and the people who review its output.

Recreate that workflow in the alternative. Reconnect the necessary tools, set access and approval rules, and ask the same people to use it. Compare the result, the handoff and the effort required to run it again.

For an agency, a client-reporting workflow can be a starting point. For an internal team, it might be competitor research or a recurring operations report. Keep the scope small enough to see where the new workspace changes the process.

Moving instructions and files is only part of a migration. GPT settings, project history, memory, integrations and permission rules do not necessarily transfer automatically.
