The best company brain software and organizational memory tools in 2026 are **[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)**, **[Dust](https://dust.tt)**, **[Slite](https://slite.com)**, **[Sento](https://www.sentohq.com)**, **[Sentra](https://www.sentra.app)**, **[Colrows](https://colrows.com)**, **[Glean](https://www.glean.com)**, **[Notion](https://www.notion.com)**, and **[Atlassian Rovo](https://www.atlassian.com/software/rovo)**.

Most teams start looking for company brain software when they hit the same wall: individual teammates have become productive with ChatGPT, Claude, or Cursor on their own laptops, but everything they teach their AI stays trapped in private chat histories and scattered Markdown files. Every new conversation starts from zero, every new hire asks the same questions in Slack, and when two departments ask an AI assistant about a metric or product policy, they get two different answers.

Choosing the right platform comes down to four practical questions:

1. **Does the software only answer questions, or does it also execute work?** Many tools stop at search and Q&A: they retrieve a paragraph from Google Drive or expose a read-only [Model Context Protocol (MCP)](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/) endpoint, leaving your team to do the actual work manually. By contrast, an **[open-source AI coworker platform](https://agenta.ai)** like **Agenta** pairs shared institutional memory (**AI Drive** and [persistent shared agent files](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/files-and-knowledge)) with real execution harnesses ([Claude Code](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/overview), [Codex](https://agenta.ai/docs/changelog/codex-harness), and [Pi](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/harnesses-and-models)) where each agent gets its own isolated computer and browser sandbox to research, draft deliverables, update files, and run scheduled or event-driven workflows.
2. **Who on your team can build, steer, and maintain the brain?** Developer-only memory daemons require terminal commands and SQL migrations, while node-and-edge workflow builders require API debugging. If you want marketing, operations, customer support, sales, product, and agency teams to contribute knowledge and build their own specialized coworkers without waiting on engineering, the workspace has to work in plain language.
3. **How does the system decide what is true when sources conflict?** Auto-ingest tools sweep every Slack message, Zoom call, and email into a timeline graph, which is great for tracking verbal commitments but risky when an outdated Slack brainstorm overrides an approved policy. Governed brains assign owners, verification states, or explicit approval gates so agents know the difference between canonical truth and raw chatter.
4. **What happens to your bill and data sovereignty as the whole company joins?** Per-seat tools charging $20 to $30+ per user per month become expensive as soon as you invite casual readers across the company, and closed cloud SaaS platforms lock your institutional memory inside proprietary databases. Permissive **[MIT-licensed](https://opensource.org/license/mit)** platforms like **Agenta** let you self-host for free or run on Cloud with **unlimited users and no per-seat tax** starting at $29/month.

## The 5 Types of Company Brain Software in 2026

The phrase "company brain" gets used by five very different categories of software. Understanding how these architectures split saves weeks of evaluating tools that solve the wrong half of the problem. (If you want a step-by-step architectural blueprint for structuring your institutional memory layers, see our companion guide on [how to build a company AI brain](/blog/how-to-build-a-company-ai-brain).)

![How Company Brain Software Splits Across Five Architectures](/blog/best-company-brain-software/company-brain-software-categories.webp)

### 1. Operational AI Coworker & Company Brain Platforms ([Agenta](https://agenta.ai), [Dust](https://dust.tt))

These platforms treat the company brain not as a passive filing cabinet, but as the shared working memory of active AI coworkers. In **[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)**, every agent combines **[persistent shared agent files](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/files-and-knowledge)** (AI Drive), [plain-language instructions](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/instructions), and [reusable skills](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/skills) with a full execution harness ([Claude Code](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/overview), [Codex](https://agenta.ai/docs/changelog/codex-harness), or [Pi](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/harnesses-and-models)) and its own isolated computer and browser sandbox. Non-technical teammates in marketing, operations, support, or sales can ask an agent to research a market, update a shared playbook, generate a report, or run a daily 6:00 AM automation, and the agent updates its durable knowledge files as it works. **Dust** takes a cloud-hosted workspace approach, connecting company data sources into permissioned Spaces that custom `@agent` assistants query inside chat and Slack.

### 2. Governed Context Layers & Self-Maintaining Knowledge Bases ([Slite](https://slite.com), [Sento](https://www.sentohq.com), [Colrows](https://colrows.com))

These tools focus on curing knowledge drift: the moment when your wiki says one thing, your Slack says another, and your data warehouse says a third.

- **Slite** pairs a collaborative wiki with **Slite Agent**, which monitors Slack, Linear, GitHub, and codebases for changes, drafts documentation updates, and routes them to a human owner to approve.
- **Sento** (**Sento Foundation**) acts as a multiplayer semantic layer where every company term, metric, list, and skill is a typed entry with a named human owner, an expiration timer, and strict read/write rules, exposed over MCP to whatever AI clients your team already uses.
- **Colrows** builds a governed semantic graph across structured SQL warehouses (Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Postgres) and unstructured documents (S3, Google Drive, OneDrive) so analytics and document queries resolve deterministically.

### 3. Auto-Ingest Organizational Memory Graphs ([Sentra](https://www.sentra.app))

Instead of asking humans to curate pages first, auto-ingest systems connect directly to meetings, Slack channels, emails, calendars, pull requests, and tickets to compile a continuous timeline of who decided what, why priorities shifted, and which commitments are still open. This works well for executive visibility, pre-meeting briefs, and handoffs, though teams still need a governed place for canonical policies so raw meeting chatter is not mistaken for final truth.

### 4. Enterprise Search & Bundled Workspace Suites ([Glean](https://www.glean.com), [Notion](https://www.notion.com), [Atlassian Rovo](https://www.atlassian.com/software/rovo))

If your company already has thousands of documents spread across Google Workspace, SharePoint, Jira, Confluence, Salesforce, and Slack, enterprise search and suite-bundled brains index those repositories while respecting existing access control lists (ACLs). **Glean** leads for independent 100+ connector enterprise search across large organizations; **Notion** combines its wiki and relational databases with Notion Agent and Enterprise Search; and **Atlassian Rovo** activates the Teamwork Graph across Jira, Confluence, and Bitbucket.

### 5. DIY Open-Source Markdown & Postgres Vaults ([GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain), [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md))

For solo technical founders and engineers who want to wire their own local or VPS-hosted memory daemon, tools like Y Combinator President Garry Tan's MIT-licensed **[GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain)** (Postgres + `pgvector` + Markdown entity pages exposed over MCP) or an **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md)** Markdown vault give direct file and database control. They are great personal or single-operator brains, though rolling them out across non-technical departments requires building your own multi-user UI, sandboxes, and approval workflows.

## Passive Wiki & Retrieval vs. an Operational Company Brain

Before diving into individual tool reviews, it helps to look at why so many internal knowledge initiatives stall after month two. When a company brain is just a search bar over static documents, humans still have to read the retrieved chunks, synthesize the answer, open five tabs, run the scripts, and remember to update the wiki afterward.

![Passive Wiki & Retrieval vs. Operational AI Coworker Company Brain](/blog/best-company-brain-software/passive-wiki-vs-operational-company-brain.webp)

An operational company brain closes that loop. When an agent runs inside a real computer and browser sandbox with access to **[persistent shared agent files](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/files-and-knowledge)**, it reads the team's current positioning, pricing, and playbooks before starting a task, executes the work across connected tools, and writes verified findings back into the shared drive (with human approval gates where required) so the next run starts smarter.

### 4. Sento: Best Governed Multiplayer Context Layer for MCP Stacks

**[Sento](https://www.sentohq.com)** builds **Sento Foundation**, a governed multiplayer context layer designed for companies where employees already use tools like Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, and Codex, and want a single, structured source of company truth that every assistant reads over MCP.

![Sento Foundation multiplayer governed context layer](/blog/best-company-brain-software/sento.webp)

#### How Sento Works as a Company Brain

Instead of treating company knowledge as long-form wiki essays or raw chat dumps, Sento structures institutional memory into discrete, typed entries:

- **Typed, Owned Entries with Expiration Rules:** Every piece of context in Sento Foundation has a defined type (`Metric`, `List`, or `Text`), a named human owner, and an explicit expiration schedule (`Daily`, `Weekly`, `Monthly`, or `None`). For example, a `DAU` metric entry carries its exact formula, what counts as good or bad, its owner, and a daily expiration flag fed by PostHog, while a `Renewal risk` list is owned by revenue operations and refreshed weekly.
- **Granular Per-Entry Read/Write Rules:** Each entry specifies which humans, agents, and scheduled routines have read access and write access (connecting systems like PostHog, HubSpot, GitHub, Granola meeting transcripts, Intercom support tickets, and Google Analytics).
- **Missing-Knowledge Feedback Loop:** When an agent queries Sento over MCP for a term, metric, or policy that has not been defined yet, Sento logs the missing-knowledge request rather than guessing, showing domain owners exactly which gaps to fill next.

#### License & Pricing

- **License & Hosting:** Proprietary cloud platform with MCP distribution and full read/write version logging.
- **Pricing:** Custom team onboarding (available via direct access request at `sentohq.com`).

#### Pros & Cons

- **Pros:** High signal-to-noise ratio because every metric and term is typed, versioned, and owned by a specific person; works natively with existing MCP clients (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex); explicit expiration timers prevent silent metric drift.
- **Cons:** Requires deliberate initial structuring of company terms and metrics; does not provide a self-hosted open-source edition or an all-in-one browser/computer sandbox for non-technical teams who do not already run their own AI clients.

## What About DIY Open-Source Vaults Like GBrain and Obsidian?

If you read technical Twitter or Hacker News, you have likely seen founders building personal or small-team brains using **[GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain)** or **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md)**:

- **[GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain) (MIT License, 31k+ GitHub stars):** Created by Y Combinator President Garry Tan to power his OpenClaw and Hermes agents, GBrain combines a Postgres + `pgvector` database with structured Markdown entity pages (separating compiled truth from an append-only chronological timeline) and exposes the brain over MCP (`gbrain mcp expose`) or via the hosted `gbrain.io` service.
- **[Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) + Filesystem MCP:** A local folder of Markdown notes with wikilinks and frontmatter, connected to Claude Desktop or Cursor via a local MCP server.

**When DIY vaults work well:** If you are a solo founder, researcher, or developer who wants a personal second brain inside your terminal or IDE, starting with Markdown files or GBrain is fast and educational.

**Where DIY vaults hit a wall for company-wide rollout:** As soon as you want your marketing lead, customer support team, operations manager, and sales reps to collaborate on the same company brain, a raw CLI daemon or local Markdown folder breaks down. Non-technical teammates do not run `pgvector` migrations or debug local MCP JSON files. That is why teams graduating from a solo vault move to an **[open-source AI coworker platform](https://agenta.ai)** like **Agenta** (which preserves the clean, inspectable Markdown file model inside shared **AI Drive** folders while wrapping it in a multi-user web workspace, isolated computer/browser sandboxes, Slack/Telegram/WhatsApp channels, and human approval gates) or a governed context layer like **Slite** or **Sento**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is company brain software?

Company brain software is a shared institutional memory and context layer that captures how an organization works (its product facts, metrics, decisions, customer history, and operating procedures), keeps that context accurate over time, and makes it usable by both human teammates and AI agents.

### What is the difference between a company wiki and an operational company brain?

A traditional company wiki is a passive library of pages that requires manual searching and quickly goes stale when nobody owns updates. An operational company brain pairs governed, persistent knowledge with active AI agents or MCP servers that read the shared context, flag outdated facts, answer questions with citations, and execute recurring workflows across Slack, GitHub, CRMs, and browsers.

### What is the best open-source company brain software in 2026?

**[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)** is the best open-source company brain and AI coworker platform for teams. Released under the permissive **[MIT License](https://opensource.org/license/mit)** and self-hostable via [Docker Compose](https://docs.docker.com/compose/) or [Kubernetes Helm](https://helm.sh/), Agenta gives non-technical and technical teammates shared persistent agent files (AI Drive), reusable skills, and real execution harnesses ([Claude Code](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/overview), [Codex](https://agenta.ai/docs/changelog/codex-harness), and [Pi](https://agenta.ai/docs/concepts/harnesses-and-models)) with isolated computer and browser sandboxes. For solo technical founders who only want a headless Postgres and Markdown daemon, **[GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain)** is a popular MIT-licensed option.

### Should we choose an auto-ingest memory tool or a governed company brain?

Auto-ingest tools like **[Sentra](https://www.sentra.app)** are helpful when your biggest pain is losing track of verbal commitments across meetings, Slack threads, and emails. However, because raw chat and meeting transcripts include abandoned ideas and unverified opinions, most teams pair or prefer a governed company brain (such as **[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)**, **[Sento](https://www.sentohq.com)**, **[Slite](https://slite.com)**, or **[Colrows](https://colrows.com)**) where canonical product facts, definitions, and playbooks have explicit human ownership and approval gates.

### How much does company brain software cost for a growing team?

Per-seat wikis and AI workspaces charge $10 to $30+ per user per month (for example, Slite Pro at $20/user/month, Notion Business at $20/user/month, and Dust Pro at EUR 24 to EUR 30/seat/month), while enterprise search platforms like Glean and Colrows require custom annual contracts. **[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)** offers a free forever self-hosted open-source edition and a Cloud Starter plan at **$29/month total with unlimited team members and no per-seat fees**.
