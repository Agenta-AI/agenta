When European and global teams outgrow generic ChatGPT subscriptions, [Langdock](https://www.langdock.com/) and [Dust](https://dust.tt/) are two of the most common platforms on the shortlist. Both promise access to frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Mistral, both support the [Model Context Protocol (MCP)](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/), and both offer European data residency with enterprise compliance certifications.

Look past the surface checklist, however, and Langdock and Dust are built around two very different mental models of how a company adopts AI:

- **Langdock** starts from the **compliance-first multi-model employee workspace**. Headquartered in Berlin, it gives every employee a governed chat interface, prompt library, reusable **Skills**, meeting note transcription, and custom **Agents**, while keeping background automations inside a separate visual **Workflows** product (`2,500` to `10,000` included runs per month, or `€449/month` billed annually for the `40,000`-run Workflows Business add-on where model tokens are billed separately). Its pricing (`€23.20/user/month` on Core or `€31.20/user/month` on Work for the first 50 standard seats) relies on rolling **5-hour session windows and Monday-to-Monday weekly windows**, and its self-hosted Kubernetes option requires at least **5,000 seats**.
- **Dust (`dust.tt`)** starts from **company-knowledge agents and collaborative workspaces**. Founded in Paris and San Francisco, Dust organizes internal knowledge into permissioned **Spaces** (`Open`, `Restricted`, and `Project` Spaces), lets teammates chain specialized `@agents` inside shared **Pods**, and renders live interactive React apps and dashboards called **Frames** that refresh on wake-up schedules. Yet its **Business plan** (`€24/seat/month` Pro or `€120/seat/month` Max billed annually, up to 100 seats) caps the entire workspace at **3 managed data-source connectors, 5 Spaces, and 5 remote MCP servers** until you upgrade to a 100+ seat Enterprise contract.
- **Neither Langdock nor Dust is open source, and neither gives every agent a real coding-agent harness or full Linux workstation.** For teams that want plain-language agent building for non-technical teammates without per-seat pricing cliffs, connector caps, or 5,000-seat self-hosting minimums, **[Agenta](https://agenta.ai)** provides a third architectural path: an [MIT-licensed](https://opensource.org/license/mit) open-source AI coworker platform where every agent runs on a battle-tested coding agent harness ([Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview), [OpenAI Codex](https://developers.openai.com/codex), or [Pi](https://github.com/badlogic/pi-mono/)) inside an isolated [Daytona](https://www.daytona.io/) Linux and browser sandbox.

## How Langdock Actually Works: EU Multi-Model Workspace + Separate Workflow Builder

![Langdock multi-model enterprise AI workspace interface](/blog/langdock-vs-dust/langdock.webp)

Founded in Berlin in 2023, [Langdock](https://www.langdock.com/) built its reputation as the GDPR-native European alternative to ChatGPT Team and Microsoft Copilot (for a broader look at employee chat portals, see our guide to the [best ChatGPT Business alternatives](/blog/best-chatgpt-business-alternatives)). Rather than forcing a company onto a single model vendor, Langdock gives administrators one governed workspace over OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Mistral, and custom endpoints.

### 1. Chat, Custom Agents, Skills, and Canvas Apps

Inside the daily employee interface, Langdock combines four workspace primitives:

- **Multi-Model Chat and Projects:** Employees switch models mid-conversation or let Langdock route queries, organize threads and reference documents inside shared **Projects**, and use built-in **Meeting Notes** transcription (`10 transcribed meetings per member per month` on Core; unlimited on Work subject to a `60 hours/week` fair-use threshold).
- **Custom [Agents](https://www.langdock.com/products/agents) and Reusable Skills:** Non-technical teammates create custom assistants with system instructions, attached knowledge bases, web search, sandboxed data analysis, and connected tool actions. Builders can also package recurring prompt patterns and instructions as reusable **Skills** that any agent or employee can invoke.
- **Interactive Canvas Apps:** Agents can render interactive mini web apps and visual artifacts on a side-by-side Canvas. On the **Core** plan, sharing a Canvas app is limited to `5 users`; the **Work** plan unlocks unlimited internal app sharing.
- **57 Native Integrations and MCP:** Langdock ships `57` built-in integrations spanning `754` pre-built actions (including Slack, Jira, Confluence, HubSpot, Linear, Google Drive, SharePoint, and GitHub), alongside custom OpenAPI action definitions and remote **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** server connections.

### 2. Separate Trigger-Action Workflows Module

Unlike platforms where the conversational agent and the background automation engine are the exact same runtime, Langdock separates interactive chat **Agents** from background **[Workflows](https://www.langdock.com/products/workflows)**:

- **Trigger-Action Automation:** Workflows run on schedules, incoming webhooks, or app events, executing up to `2,000 steps per workflow run` across APIs and LLM prompts.
- **Included Run Quotas vs the €449/month Business Add-On:** As documented in [Langdock's pricing guide](https://docs.langdock.com/en/admin/billing/pricing), the Core plan includes `2,500 workflow runs per workspace per month` (`5` runs/minute rate limit) and the Work plan includes `10,000 workflow runs per workspace per month` (`20` runs/minute). Teams running heavier background automation must add the **Workflows Business** package at **€449 per workspace per month** (billed annually) for `40,000 runs per month`, with additional 10,000-run packs priced at `€100/month`.
- **Separate Token Billing on Workflows Business:** A critical detail in Langdock's billing documentation is that while standard chat and interactive agent turns draw from each user's seat allowance, **AI model usage inside the Workflows Business add-on is billed separately through Langdock's API token pricing** (or your own BYOK keys).

### 3. Rolling 5-Hour and Weekly Usage Windows

Beginning March 24, 2026, Langdock replaced legacy flat usage caps with a two-window throttling mechanism detailed in its [Fair Usage Policy](https://docs.langdock.com/en/using-langdock/models-and-limits/fair-usage-policy):

1. **5-Hour Rolling Session Window:** Tracks active token and tool consumption across a rolling five-hour window to prevent burst spikes.
2. **Monday-to-Monday Weekly Window:** Tracks cumulative weekly consumption (resetting Mondays at `00:00 UTC`).

When a teammate hits `100%` of either their 5-hour or weekly window on a standard seat, Langdock automatically throttles their chat and agent requests down to **`GPT-6 Luna`**, a lightweight fallback model, unless the workspace admin has enabled metered **Extra Usage**, upgraded that user to a **5x usage seat** (`€99/user/month` on Core or `€109/user/month` on Work), or routed models through **Bring Your Own Key (BYOK)**.

### 4. Where Langdock Shows Its Limits

1. **Governance unbundling on Core in 2027:** While SSO and SCIM are included on Core, Langdock's **Governance** package (custom data retention rules, granular admin roles, audit logs, and custom terms of use) is only free on Core until **January 1, 2027**, after which it adds `€3.50/user/month` (or requires upgrading to Work at `€31.20/user/month`).
2. **Enterprise-only self-hosting thresholds:** Per [Langdock's pricing page](https://www.langdock.com/pricing), dedicated single-tenant cloud deployment requires at least **2,000 seats**, and self-hosted Kubernetes / Bring Your Own Cloud (BYOC) requires at least **5,000 seats**. Startups and mid-market engineering teams cannot self-host Langdock.
3. **Not a coding-agent harness:** Langdock provides sandboxed data analysis for charts and spreadsheets and Canvas previews, but agents do not operate inside a full Linux VM with `Claude Code`, `Codex`, or `Pi`, persistent git repositories, and a headless Chromium browser.

## How Dust (`dust.tt`) Actually Works: Permissioned Spaces, Multi-Agent Pods, and React Frames

![Dust enterprise AI agent platform and knowledge workspace](/blog/langdock-vs-dust/dust.webp)

Where Langdock begins with employee chat and bolts on workflows, [Dust](https://dust.tt/) (`dust.tt`) treats **company knowledge synchronization and composable `@agents`** as the center of gravity.

### 1. Permissioned Knowledge Spaces (`Open`, `Restricted`, `Project`)

In Dust, agents do not simply search a flat workspace folder. Administrators and builders organize synced company data sources (Notion, Slack, Google Drive, Confluence, GitHub, Intercom, Zendesk, and SharePoint) into **[Spaces](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/spaces)**:

- **Company Space (`Open`):** Accessible to everyone in the workspace for company-wide policies, handbooks, and shared documentation.
- **Restricted Spaces:** Scoped to specific groups (such as Finance, Legal, or Executive teams) or provisioned automatically via SCIM on Enterprise plans so sensitive folders never leak into general agent retrieval.
- **Project Spaces:** Dedicated context boundaries for cross-functional initiatives where teammates combine synced folders, static uploads, and specialized agents.

### 2. Multi-Agent `@mention` Orchestration, MCP Tools, and Warehouse SQL

Every assistant in Dust is an `@agent` that combines three building blocks:

- **Instructions and Frontier Models:** Builders write natural-language instructions and pick from 20+ models across Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, Fireworks, and Together AI.
- **Sub-Agent Delegation and Collaborative Pods:** Inside shared **Pods** (or Slack, Microsoft Teams, and the Chrome extension), users can call multiple `@agents` in the same thread, and a primary agent can delegate subtasks to specialized child agents or trigger on recurring schedules and webhooks.
- **Remote [MCP Servers](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/mcp) and SQL Querying:** Dust built its action layer directly around the Model Context Protocol. Agents can call remote MCP servers (authenticated via OAuth, bearer tokens, or custom headers) with per-tool confirmation settings (`Always ask`, `Never ask`, or user-cached approval), and can execute structured SQL queries against connected data warehouses such as **BigQuery**, **Snowflake**, and **Salesforce**.

### 3. Interactive React Frames (`viz`)

One of Dust's most distinctive features is **[Frames](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/frames)** (`viz`):

- Instead of returning static Markdown tables, a Dust agent can generate an **interactive React component** (dashboards, sortable tables, charts, or mini internal tools) rendered directly inside a Dust conversation or pinned to a shared **Pod**.
- Paired with Dust's **wake-up schedules**, an agent can wake up every morning, query Snowflake and HubSpot via MCP, refresh the live React Frame in the team's Pod, and post a summary notification.

### 4. Where Dust Shows Its Limits

Despite its strong knowledge and dashboard ergonomics, Dust's packaging introduces sharp constraints for growing teams:

1. **The 3-Connector, 5-Space, 5-MCP Cap on Business:** According to [Dust's pricing page](https://dust.tt/home/pricing), the **Business plan** (which covers teams up to 100 seats) limits the entire workspace to **at most 3 managed data-source connectors, 5 Spaces, and 5 remote MCP servers**. If a 25-person company wants to sync Notion, Slack, Google Drive, GitHub, and Zendesk (`5` connectors) or segment data across `8` department Spaces, they hit the Business plan ceiling and are forced toward a 100+ seat Enterprise conversation.
2. **Expiring Per-Seat Credits (No Pooling on Business):** On the Business plan, **Pro** seats (`€24/seat/month` billed annually or `€30` monthly) receive `8,000 credits/month` and **Max** seats (`€120/seat/month` billed annually or `€150` monthly) receive `40,000 credits/month`. Unused monthly credits **do not roll over**, and workspace-wide credit pooling is locked behind the Enterprise tier.
3. **Closed-Source Cloud SaaS with No General Linux VM:** Dust is proprietary SaaS with no open-source or self-hosted community edition. Furthermore, while Dust renders front-end React Frames and runs warehouse queries, it does not give each agent a persistent Linux VM, shell, file system, and headless browser driven by a coding-agent harness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the main difference between Langdock and Dust?

Langdock is designed first as a GDPR-compliant European multi-model AI workspace for replacing ChatGPT Team across an organization, pairing employee chat, custom agents, skills, and meeting transcription with a separate visual workflow builder. Dust (dust.tt) is designed around company-knowledge agents grounded in permissioned Spaces, multi-agent @mention delegation, collaborative Pods, and interactive React dashboards called Frames.

### How does Langdock pricing compare to Dust pricing in 2026?

Langdock charges €23.20 per user per month on Core (or €99.00 per user per month for a 5x usage seat) and €31.20 per user per month on Work (or €109.00 per user per month for 5x usage) for the first 50 seats, plus an optional €449 per month Workflows Business add-on (billed annually) for 40,000 monthly workflow runs where workflow LLM tokens are billed separately via API pricing. Dust charges €24 per seat per month billed annually (€30 monthly) for Pro seats with 8,000 monthly credits, or €120 per seat per month billed annually (€150 monthly) for Max seats with 40,000 monthly credits, and allows up to 5 free seats on a Business workspace.

### What are the connector and usage limits on Langdock and Dust?

Langdock enforces dual rolling usage limits on included chat and agent models: a 5-hour session window and a Monday-to-Monday weekly window, falling back to GPT-6 Luna once a user hits 100% unless Extra Usage or BYOK is enabled. Dust's Business plan (up to 100 seats) caps workspaces at 3 managed data-source connectors, 5 Spaces, and 5 remote MCP servers, and unused monthly credits do not roll over.

### Can you self-host Langdock or Dust?

Neither platform offers a self-hosted edition for startups or mid-market teams. Dust is a proprietary cloud-only SaaS platform with US and EU data residency options and single-tenant cloud deployment on Enterprise (100+ seats). Langdock offers multi-tenant EU SaaS by default, requires at least 2,000 seats for dedicated single-tenant cloud hosting, and requires at least 5,000 seats for self-hosted Kubernetes (BYOC) deployment.

### Do Langdock or Dust run coding agent harnesses like Claude Code or Codex?

No. Langdock runs a proprietary chat-and-agent orchestration layer with sandboxed data analysis, Canvas apps, and a separate trigger-action workflow builder. Dust runs a proprietary knowledge-and-MCP agent runtime with SQL warehouse querying and interactive React Frames. Neither platform runs open-source coding agent harnesses such as Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, or Pi inside a full per-agent Linux virtual machine.

### What is the best open-source alternative to Langdock and Dust?

Agenta (MIT-licensed) is an open-source AI coworker platform that lets non-technical and technical teams build plain-language AI coworkers powered by real coding agent harnesses (Claude Code, Codex, and Pi) inside isolated Daytona Linux and Chromium sandboxes. Unlike Langdock and Dust, Agenta can be self-hosted free from 1 user up via Docker Compose or Helm with SSO and RBAC included, or used on Agenta Cloud starting at $0 on Hobby and $29 per month on Starter with unlimited team members and zero per-seat fees.
