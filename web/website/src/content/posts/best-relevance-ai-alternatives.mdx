## What is Relevance AI?

Relevance AI is a cloud platform designed around a three-layer hierarchy: **Tools**, **Agents**, and **Workforce**.

1. **Tools** are deterministic step sequences that combine prompt blocks, API calls, web scrapers, Python or JavaScript snippets, and knowledge table lookups.
2. **Agents** wrap those tools with system instructions, memory, triggers, and human approval rules so the agent can decide which tool to run for a given task.
3. **Workforce** is a visual canvas where multiple specialized agents hand tasks to one another through conditional edges, such as an inbound research agent passing enriched account notes to an outreach drafting agent.

![Relevance AI Workforce canvas showing multi-agent handoffs, tool nodes, and conditional routing](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/relevance-ai.webp)
_Relevance AI lets teams connect specialized agents and custom tools on a visual Workforce canvas._

This architecture makes Relevance AI popular with go-to-market (GTM) teams and sales operations specialists. It ships with prebuilt BDR, lifecycle marketing, and customer research agents, native connections to HubSpot, Salesforce, Gmail, and Slack, and a visual builder that lets non-engineers inspect how each tool step is wired.

## Why teams look for a Relevance AI alternative

Relevance AI works well when your use case matches its visual Workforce canvas and your team fits comfortably inside its seat and credit tiers. Teams typically evaluate alternatives when they run into four operational friction points.

### 1. Reconciling Actions and Vendor Credits

Relevance AI retired its legacy free plan in late 2025 (September 29, 2025 for new sign-ups and October 20, 2025 for existing users) and uses a two-meter billing model documented in its [pricing guide](https://relevanceai.com/docs/get-started/relevance-pricing):

- **Actions** measure platform execution. Every time an agent runs a tool step or completes an agent step, it consumes Actions. Additional Action top-ups cost **$80 per 1,000 Actions**.
- **Vendor Credits** cover the underlying cost of language models and third-party data providers when you use Relevance AI's managed credentials.

You can bring your own API keys (BYOK) from OpenAI or Anthropic to avoid consuming Vendor Credits, but every step in a multi-agent run still consumes Actions. When a single research task triggers an orchestrator agent, three sub-agents, and eight tool steps, estimating monthly Action consumption becomes harder than tracking model token spend or full workflow runs.

### 2. The pricing jump from Pro to Team for startups and agencies

On [Relevance AI's pricing page](https://relevanceai.com/pricing), the entry **Pro** plan costs **$19 per month billed annually** ($29 billed monthly) and includes 7,000 Actions per month, **2 Build Users**, and **1 project**.

Once a startup needs a third teammate to edit prompts or tools, or an automation agency needs separate projects to isolate client workspaces, the next tier is **Team** at **$234 per month billed annually** ($349 billed monthly) for 5 Build Users, 45 End Users, and 5 shared projects. For bootstrapped teams and agencies delivering workflows to clients, jumping from $19 to $234 per month just to add a third builder or a second project is often the main reason to compare other platforms.

### 3. Maintaining multi-agent graphs versus working with a coworker in Slack

Visual multi-agent graphs look clean in a demo, but maintaining a five-agent Workforce canvas in production requires constant upkeep. When an output looks wrong, someone has to trace which sub-agent misfired, which edge condition triggered, and which prompt version was active.

Many teams discover they do not actually need five separate sub-agents passing JSON back and forth. They need one well-configured AI coworker that holds their company context, uses a clear set of skills and tools, and works directly inside a shared Slack channel where teammates can read the thread, reply with a correction, and improve the instructions in place.

### 4. Cloud-only hosting and model harness lock-in

Relevance AI runs as a managed cloud platform, with enterprise data residency options available on custom contracts. Teams that want to self-host the workspace on their own servers, hand a self-hosted instance over to a client, or run coding and research harnesses such as Claude Code, Codex, or Pi against their own repositories need an open-source architecture.

## How to choose the right Relevance AI alternative

Before comparing feature lists, decide which product shape matches the way your team works. Most tools in this category fall into one of three operating models:

![Diagram comparing three product shapes for Relevance AI alternatives: visual multi-agent workforces, conversational coworker workspaces, and deterministic node workflows](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/relevance-ai-alternatives-product-shapes.webp)

When evaluating alternatives with your team, test four practical questions:

- **Who builds and maintains the agent?** If non-engineering operators and founders will refine instructions every week, look for plain-language instructions (`AGENTS.md`), reusable skills, and built-in version history rather than complex node graphs.
- **How does pricing scale as more teammates join?** Check whether adding a viewer, a Slack participant, or a third builder forces a tier upgrade, and whether bringing your own API keys eliminates platform step charges.
- **Where does the human review happen?** Decide whether you want teammates reviewing agent work inside a separate dashboard or inside existing Slack channels and threads.
- **Do you need self-hosting or client handoff?** If you run an agency or handle sensitive internal data, verify whether you can self-host the platform under an open-source license or invite unlimited collaborators at a flat monthly rate.

## 1. Agenta: best open-source alternative for shared AI coworkers and low-code automations

[Agenta](https://agenta.ai/) is an open-source, low-code platform where teams build, share, and govern persistent AI coworkers and recurring automations. It combines the low-code speed of a visual agent builder like Gumloop with the collaborative workspace of a teammate in Slack, without requiring you to wire brittle node graphs for every step.

Instead of splitting work across a tangle of single-purpose sub-agents, you configure an Agenta coworker with plain-language instructions (`AGENTS.md`), reusable skills, durable workspace files (`agent-files/`), and connected tools. Your coworker can chat in the web workspace, answer mentions in Slack channels, run on a cron schedule, or react to external webhook and integration events.

![Agenta workspace showing a configured SEO Content Agent alongside its durable file tree and interactive preview](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/agenta.webp)
_Agenta lets teams configure persistent AI coworkers with instructions, skills, connected tools, durable files, and live app views._

### Why teams choose Agenta over Relevance AI

- **Slack coworker integration:** You can publish an Agenta agent directly to Slack and invite it into channels like `#market-research`, `#support`, or `#revops`. Anyone in the channel can mention the coworker, review its draft in the thread, and steer its next step, rather than only receiving one-way escalation notifications.
- **Versioning of instructions, skills, and tools:** Every time a founder, operator, or engineer updates the agent's instructions, skills, or tool permissions, Agenta commits a versioned revision. You can compare versions side by side and see which configuration produced a specific result.
- **Cost and trace analytics:** Every chat turn, scheduled run, and event trigger leaves an auditable trace showing tool calls, latency, token counts, and estimated model cost. You do not have to reconcile separate Action and Vendor Credit meters to understand what an agent costs.
- **Vendor, model, and harness freedom:** Choose models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and other providers per agent, and run agentic harnesses including Claude Code, Codex, and Pi.
- **Works with an existing ChatGPT subscription (local self-hosted personal use):** If you self-host Agenta locally for your own personal use, you can authenticate through a mounted ChatGPT-compatible harness login rather than paying per API token. For Agenta Cloud or shared team deployments, you use included cloud credits or bring your own provider API keys.
- **Self-hosting and unlimited seats on paid cloud plans:** Agenta's core is MIT-licensed open source, making it straightforward to self-host on your own infrastructure or deploy for a client. On Agenta Cloud, the Starter plan costs $29 per month and includes unlimited team members with no per-seat fee.

**Best for:** Startups, bootstrapped teams, and automation agencies that want shared AI coworkers in Slack, versioned low-code configuration, clear run cost analytics, unlimited team seats, and the option to self-host.

**Pricing:** Free cloud tier with $10 in included credits and up to 3 members; **Starter** at **$29 per month** with $30 in included credits and **unlimited team members**; **Plus** at **$99 per month** with $110 in included credits and unlimited team members; or **free self-hosted open source** (MIT license).

**Pros**

- Persistent AI coworkers that work in Slack threads, web chat, cron schedules, and event triggers.
- Built-in version history for instructions, skills, and connected tools so non-engineers and engineers can iterate safely.
- Unlimited team seats on the $29/month Starter cloud plan, removing the 2-builder bottleneck on Relevance AI Pro.
- Full trace and token cost visibility on every run without dual Action and Vendor Credit meters.
- MIT-licensed open-source self-hosting for internal data control or client handoffs.

**Cons**

- Designed around conversational coworkers, skills, and scheduled/event runs rather than a drag-and-drop multi-agent flowchart canvas.
- Using a personal ChatGPT subscription login requires a local self-hosted personal setup and is not available on Agenta Cloud.

**Verdict:** Choose Agenta when you want a low-code agent workspace that your whole team or client can collaborate with in Slack, with versioned configuration, clear run costs, unlimited seats, and open-source self-hosting.

## 2. Gumloop: best for visual AI workflows and GTM operations

[Gumloop](https://www.gumloop.com/) is a visual AI automation platform built around drag-and-drop workflow pipelines ("flows"), custom web scrapers, and internal AI agents ("Gummie"). If your favorite part of Relevance AI is chaining prompt steps, web enrichment, and CRM updates on a visual canvas, Gumloop is the closest visual alternative.

Gumloop combines deterministic workflow nodes with agentic steps and Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool servers. Marketing, SEO, and revenue operations teams use it to scrape competitor pages, enrich inbound leads, analyze call transcripts, and publish structured updates into Slack, Notion, or HubSpot.

![Gumloop visual canvas showing connected workflow nodes, AI prompts, and web scraping steps](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/gumloop.webp)
_Gumloop pairs a visual node canvas with built-in web scraping and AI enrichment steps._

**Best for:** GTM, marketing, and operations teams that want a visual drag-and-drop canvas for scraping, data enrichment, and multi-step AI flows without per-seat fees on paid plans.

**Pricing:** [Gumloop](https://www.gumloop.com/pricing) offers a **Free** plan with 5,000 credits, 1 seat, and 2 active triggers; **Pro** starts at **$37 per month** with 20,000 credits, **unlimited seats**, and unlimited workspaces; **Team** starts at **$244 per month** with 75,000 credits; and Enterprise uses custom pricing. You can bring your own LLM API keys on paid plans, which carries an 8% orchestration fee on provider token costs.

**Pros**

- Clean visual node builder that is approachable for non-engineering GTM and operations teams.
- Pro plan ($37/month) includes unlimited seats and workspaces, avoiding Relevance AI's jump to $234/month for a third builder.
- Strong built-in web scraping, browser automation, and document parsing nodes.
- Single credit meter with published per-node credit costs.

**Cons**

- Closed-source cloud SaaS with no self-hosted open-source edition.
- Bringing your own LLM API keys still incurs an 8% orchestration fee on provider token usage.
- Complex branching workflows can still consume credits quickly when running large batch jobs.

**Verdict:** Choose Gumloop if you want to stay with a visual drag-and-drop AI workflow builder for GTM and scraping tasks, while getting unlimited team seats at a lower starting price than Relevance AI Team.

## 3. n8n: best for deterministic node-based workflow automation

[n8n](https://n8n.io/) is a source-available workflow automation platform that blends deterministic app integrations, custom JavaScript and Python code nodes, and LangChain-based AI agent nodes. Where Relevance AI treats almost everything as an agent or tool step billed per Action, n8n treats AI as one node inside a predictable automation pipeline.

That distinction matters for cost and reliability. On n8n, a 25-step workflow that fetches a webhook, queries Postgres, calls an AI agent node with three tools, and updates HubSpot counts as **one workflow execution**, regardless of how many internal steps run inside that execution.

![n8n workflow editor showing an AI Agent node connected to an Anthropic chat model, Postgres memory, and tool nodes](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/n8n.webp)
_n8n lets technical teams combine deterministic workflow nodes, custom code, and LangChain AI agent steps._

**Best for:** Technical operators, developers, and automation agencies that want deterministic node-based workflows, custom code steps, per-execution billing, and full self-hosting.

**Pricing:** Free self-hosted **Community Edition** (under the Sustainable Use License); [n8n Cloud](https://n8n.io/pricing/) **Starter** costs **€20 per month billed annually** (€24 monthly) for 2,500 workflow executions with unlimited steps and unlimited users; **Pro** costs **€50 per month billed annually** for 10,000 executions; and **Business** costs **€667 per month billed annually** for 40,000 executions. You connect your own LLM API keys directly with no n8n markup.

**Pros**

- Bills per complete workflow execution on Cloud (or free on self-hosted hardware) rather than charging per internal step.
- Over 400 native integrations plus full HTTP request, webhook, JavaScript, and Python code nodes.
- Direct BYOK model connections with zero markup on your OpenAI or Anthropic token spend.
- Self-hosted Community Edition is widely used by agencies and startups to keep infrastructure costs low.

**Cons**

- Steeper learning curve for non-technical business users who prefer plain-language agent instructions over JSON data mapping.
- Does not provide a native conversational coworker workspace out of the box unless you wire your own chat or Slack bot triggers.
- The Sustainable Use License restricts certain commercial redistributions where n8n is offered as a hosted product to third parties.

**Verdict:** Choose n8n when your workflows are 80% deterministic data routing and 20% AI reasoning, and you want predictable per-execution pricing or self-hosted control.

## 5. Dify: best for RAG pipelines and visual LLM app workflows

[Dify](https://dify.ai/) is an open-source LLM application development platform that combines a visual workflow canvas, agent nodes, prompt IDE, and a built-in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline engine. RAG is the process of indexing your own documents so an AI model can retrieve relevant passages before answering a question.

Compared with Relevance AI, Dify is geared more toward product and engineering teams building internal AI tools or customer-facing LLM features. It gives you fine-grained control over document chunking, embedding models, vector databases, hybrid search, and API publishing, and can be self-hosted via Docker Compose or Kubernetes.

![Dify visual studio showing a multi-branch LLM workflow with knowledge retrieval and tool nodes](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/dify.webp)
_Dify combines visual agent workflows with document ingestion, retrieval tuning, and API publishing._

**Best for:** Engineering and product teams that need an open-source visual builder for RAG knowledge pipelines, structured workflows, and API-backed LLM applications.

**Pricing:** Free self-hosted **Community Edition**; on [Dify Cloud](https://dify.ai/pricing), **Sandbox** is free (200 OpenAI calls, 1 member); **Professional** costs **$59 per workspace per month** (3 team members, 50 apps, 5 GB knowledge storage); and **Team** costs **$159 per workspace per month** (50 team members, 200 apps, 20 GB knowledge storage). You plug in your own model API keys directly.

**Pros**

- Strong visual RAG pipeline builder for chunking, indexing, and testing document retrieval.
- Open-source Community Edition can be self-hosted with your own model providers and vector stores.
- Workspace-level cloud pricing ($59/month for 3 members, $159/month for 50 members) scales more gently than Relevance AI Team.
- Exposes every workflow as a clean REST API or embeddable web app.

**Cons**

- Built primarily for developers and technical builders rather than non-technical GTM operators.
- Focuses on app and API construction rather than persistent Slack coworkers that collaborate with a team in threads.
- Self-hosting at scale requires managing Docker containers, databases, and vector storage.

**Verdict:** Choose Dify when your team is building RAG-heavy internal tools or LLM APIs and wants an open-source visual studio with direct model API key control.

**Verdict:** Choose Dust when your highest priority is giving teams governed AI assistants that search and act across scattered internal company knowledge.

## How to migrate from Relevance AI without breaking operations

Moving off Relevance AI does not require rebuilding every tool and sub-agent over a weekend. Because platforms meter and structure multi-step runs differently, the safest approach is migrating one high-volume workflow first and comparing both its output and its run cost.

![Diagram comparing how a 5-step agent workflow is metered in Relevance AI versus Agenta, Gumloop, and n8n, alongside a 4-step migration checklist](/blog/best-relevance-ai-alternatives/relevance-ai-billing-and-migration-map.webp)

Follow four practical steps for your first workflow migration:

1. **Export your prompts, tool schemas, and knowledge tables:** Copy the system instructions from your primary Relevance AI agent, the JSON input schemas and API endpoints from its custom tools, and any CSV or table data it references.
2. **Collapse unnecessary sub-agents into reusable skills:** In Relevance AI, teams often create separate sub-agents just to keep prompt context manageable. In a workspace like [Agenta](https://agenta.ai/), you can turn those sub-agents into modular skills and playbooks under one coworker so there is a single configuration and version history to maintain.
3. **Reconnect triggers and human review channels:** Wire your schedule or webhook trigger, and connect your Slack workspace so teammates review drafts and approve actions directly in a channel thread.
4. **Compare 10 real runs side by side:** Run the same 10 inputs through both systems. Check output accuracy, failure recovery, and the exact cost recorded in the run trace before turning off the old Relevance AI trigger.
