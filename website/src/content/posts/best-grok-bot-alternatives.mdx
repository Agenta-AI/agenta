*Compare Grok Bot alternatives for one-off tasks, persistent AI coworkers, team work, personal assistance, model choice, and self-hosting.*

[Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) is a system for creating named AI teammates that keep working after you step away. Each Bot has memory, tools, files, routines, and access to a persistent cloud computer. Bots can work across websites and applications, coordinate with one another, and learn how you prefer a job to be done.

Grok Bot is xAI's persistent-agent product. It is separate from the Grok chatbot and Grok's image-generation features.

That makes Grok Bot different from an ordinary chat with ChatGPT or Claude. A chat helps with the request in front of you. Grok Bot is organized around persistent agents that can take responsibility for ongoing work.

You may still want an alternative. Some products are better for one polished task. Some focus on research or visual automation. Personal assistants give technical users more control over their own devices. Team coworker platforms add visible configuration, permissions, versioning, cost controls, model choice, and self-hosting.

## Key takeaways

- Choose a task agent when you want one finished deliverable, a workflow builder when the steps are known, and an AI coworker when the responsibility continues over time.
- Grok Bot remains one of the simplest ways to create several persistent agents that share a managed computer and learn routines through use.
- Choose an alternative when you need stronger team governance, your own deployment, or more control over models, payment, and configuration.

## What makes Grok Bot different

Grok Bot is built around persistent AI coworkers rather than a list of disposable chats. A Bot has a name, job, memory, tools, files, and working context. You can give separate Bots responsibility for sales outreach, expenses, recruiting, product work, or operations, then let them coordinate in shared threads.

Each Bot has a screen on one persistent cloud computer scoped to your account. All Bots in the account share the computer's files, browser sessions, and application logins. This makes handoffs easy because one Bot can continue from another Bot's work without repeating setup. It also means the Bots do not have separate computer-level security boundaries.

Grok Bot can learn a workflow by watching you perform it. It saves the process as a routine, applies corrections, and can repeat the work later or on a schedule. This moves it beyond a task agent that starts from a fresh request each time.

## What Grok Bot does especially well

The appeal is simplicity. You create a Bot, explain its job, grant access when needed, and work with it through conversation. The platform hides much of the underlying configuration so that delegating feels closer to messaging a colleague than operating an automation system.

Its shared cloud computer also makes coordination unusually direct. One Bot can continue from files, browser sessions, and application state created by another. If you want a managed roster of agents and do not want to design their infrastructure, this is Grok Bot's strongest advantage.

## Why consider a Grok Bot alternative?

The main reasons depend on what you want to control.

- **You need a different product shape.** A polished task agent or deterministic workflow may fit better than a team of persistent Bots.
- **You want model freedom.** Grok Bot uses the models and usage system provided by xAI. Other platforms let you bring an API key, use an existing AI subscription, or route different jobs to different models.
- **You need team governance.** Professional use may require permissions, visible configuration, version history, per-agent budgets, auditability, and clearer separation between coworkers.
- **You want ownership and portability.** Open-source and self-hostable products let you control the deployment and inspect more of the system. Readable agent configuration can also make skills and instructions easier to version and move.
- **You want a different cost model.** Managed plans and credits are simple, but they may be expensive for frequent work. Using your own subscription, infrastructure, or lower-cost model can make routine jobs more predictable.

## How to choose a Grok Bot alternative

Use these questions to narrow the list.

### Do you need a task, a workflow, or a coworker?

A task agent takes a goal and returns a result. Manus, ChatGPT Work, and Claude Cowork are strong for research, files, presentations, websites, and other finished deliverables.

A workflow system runs a process whose steps are mostly known in advance. Lindy is useful when you want templates, triggers, integrations, and approvals in a visual editor.

An AI coworker owns an ongoing responsibility. It keeps context, uses tools, runs recurring work, learns preferences, and improves through feedback. Grok Bot, Agenta, and Hermes place this relationship closer to the center of the product.

![Task agents, workflow builders, personal assistants, and team AI coworkers compared by what they do and remember](/blog/best-grok-bot-alternatives/agent-product-shapes.webp)

### What can it access?

Check whether the agent has a browser, terminal, files, a sandbox, application integrations, and access to the computer where the work happens. Then check where that environment runs and how credentials are controlled.

A full computer helps when the agent must use websites with no clean application programming interface. Direct integrations are often more reliable for structured business work. The strongest option depends on the systems your job touches.

### Is it for one person or a team?

Personal agents optimize for one person's devices, accounts, channels, and preferences. Team products need shared access, permissions, separate responsibilities, visible configuration, versioning, cost controls, and a way for several people to improve the system safely.

### Can you choose the model and payment method?

Some products tie the agent to one vendor's models. Others choose several models behind the scenes. Open systems may let you bring an API key, connect a supported AI subscription, or use a free or inexpensive model for routine work.

This matters for cost and fit. A strong model may be worth using for strategy or difficult research, while a cheaper model may be enough for classification, extraction, or recurring summaries.

![Model and payment control from bundled vendor models to a user-controlled workspace](/blog/best-grok-bot-alternatives/model-payment-control.webp)

### How much configuration do you want?

A fixed product is easier to start. A code-first system offers deep control but asks you to operate more of the stack. The middle ground lets a non-engineer work with the agent through conversation while an engineer can inspect and change its instructions, tools, permissions, and runtime.

## Agenta: best for configurable AI coworkers for teams

[Agenta](https://agenta.ai/) is an open-source workspace for building AI coworkers and automations for professional work. Instead of giving one general assistant every request, you can create several coworkers with distinct jobs. A research coworker, search engine optimization coworker, outreach coworker, and customer-support coworker can each keep the context and tools needed for their responsibility.

Each coworker can have its own instructions, skills, files, tools, channels, permissions, and schedules. It can work interactively in a conversation, run in the background, or delegate parts of a job to other agents. Feedback can update the coworker's configuration, and [versioning](/blog/prompt-versioning-guide) lets the team keep the better behavior without losing the previous setup.

Agenta is designed to be simple without hiding all of the machinery. A business user can ask for work, give feedback, and reuse the coworker. A technical teammate can inspect the configuration, connect systems, set permissions, trace runs, review costs, and change how the coworker operates. This follows the same principle as [prompt management for non-engineers](/blog/prompt-management-for-non-engineers): domain experts should be able to improve behavior without losing technical control.

![Agenta coworker workspace](/blog/best-grok-bot-alternatives/agenta.webp)

Model and payment freedom are central. When self-hosting Agenta, teams can bring their own model API keys, use supported ChatGPT or Claude subscriptions, or connect supported free and lower-cost models. That lets the team match the model to the job rather than paying the same bundled rate for every task.

**Best for:** Teams that want several persistent coworkers for real business responsibilities, with visible configuration and room for both non-engineers and engineers.

**Pricing:** Agenta is free and MIT licensed for self-hosting. Costs depend on the models, subscriptions, integrations, and infrastructure you choose.

**Pros:**

- Multiple coworkers for different business jobs
- Feedback-driven improvement with visible, versioned configuration
- Team sharing, permissions, schedules, channels, and integrations
- Bring-your-own-key and supported AI subscription access
- Model choice, cost visibility, tracing, and self-hosting
- Open-source platform and portable agent components

**Cons:**

- Requires more initial configuration than Grok Bot
- A technical teammate may still be useful for advanced integrations and deployment
- The benefits are clearest for ongoing work rather than one simple task

**Verdict:** Choose Agenta when your goal is to build coworkers that become part of how a team operates. It fits teams that want a conversational experience for business users and enough depth for engineers to configure, govern, and improve the system.

## OpenClaw: best open-source personal assistant

[OpenClaw](https://openclaw.ai/) is an open-source personal AI assistant that runs on your devices and meets you in channels such as messaging applications. It can connect to hosted models, subscription-backed access, gateways, and local models.

OpenClaw is strongest when one technical user wants a deeply personal agent with control over the machine, channels, models, and extensions. Its large community and open code make it flexible, and self-hosting keeps the operating environment under the user's control.

![OpenClaw chat and agent control interface](/blog/best-grok-bot-alternatives/openclaw.webp)

That flexibility comes with responsibility. The person running OpenClaw must understand what the agent can access, how credentials are stored, which skills are enabled, and how the environment is maintained. It is centered on a personal assistant rather than a professional workspace with team roles, shared governance, and several business users improving the same coworker.

**Best for:** Technical self-hosters who want a personal assistant across their own devices and communication channels.

**Pricing:** OpenClaw is free and open source. Model, hosting, and infrastructure costs depend on the setup.

**Pros:**

- Open source and self-hostable
- Broad choice of hosted, subscription-backed, and local models
- Strong personal-channel support
- Large community and extension ecosystem
- Deep control over the runtime

**Cons:**

- Requires technical setup and ongoing maintenance
- Security depends heavily on how the operator configures access
- Team permissions, shared governance, and business-user workflows are not the center of the product

**Verdict:** Choose OpenClaw when you want to operate a powerful personal assistant yourself. It is a strong fit for technical users who value control more than a ready-made team workspace.

## How to move from Grok Bot

Start with one real responsibility rather than trying to recreate every Bot at once.

1. List each Bot's job, routines, tools, files, credentials, schedules, and approval boundaries.
2. Separate disposable tasks from ongoing responsibilities and fixed workflows.
3. Save the instructions, examples, preferred output formats, and corrections that made the Bot useful.
4. Identify which Bots share files, browser sessions, and application logins on the Grok Bot computer.
5. Choose the target product based on the job. Use a task agent for changing requests, a workflow builder for known processes, and a coworker platform for lasting responsibilities.
6. Run one real job in the old and new product with the same inputs and acceptance criteria.
7. Move credentials and permissions deliberately. Give each new agent only the access its job requires.
8. Move the strongest responsibility first, then repeat the process.

The hard part is not copying a prompt. It is preserving the working context, routines, access, preferences, and review points that made the Bot effective.

You can share a Grok Bot for someone else to copy, but that does not transfer your computer, logins, or conversation history. When moving to another product, preserve the Bot's job, instructions, routines, files, examples, permissions, and learned preferences. Open systems with readable, versioned configuration make those parts easier to inspect and maintain.
